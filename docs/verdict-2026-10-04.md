# Thirdlight — engine audit and verdict (2026-10-04)

The owner asked for this overnight: a full audit of every system, what works and what doesn't, and an
honest verdict on whether to keep Thirdlight or move to Godot/Unity. Engine at `1c24eb23`.

Method: six read-only code audits (core data, runtime, rendering, editor, assets/export/AI, process and
games), one live measurement run on the real Iris Xe GPU (copies of Skyforge and Sprout; the game repos
were not touched), a build/lint/test run, and spot checks of the key claims. The working notes are
outside the repo, in `~/.cache/thirdlight-audit/`. Effort figures are estimates, not measurements.

## 1. Bottom line

**Keep it, but stop building features for about a month and change how it is built.** Thirdlight is
not a pile of broken code. It builds clean, its tests pass, both games open and play at this commit,
and the data layer is solid. The live measurement says your platform choice is fine for your art style:
plain three.js draws Skyforge's village at **110–150 fps** on this iGPU, and Thirdlight draws the same
frame at **55 fps**. The missing ~60 % of the frame is overhead in our own code, and every source of it
is known and fixable.

What has gone wrong is the way it was built, not the idea:
- 741 commits in 18 days.
- Six format bumps in eight days, with no compatibility policy.
- The games acting as the test suite.
- Nobody, including you, has built a level in the editor by hand.

If that doesn't change, every new feature will keep breaking the games, whichever engine we use.

The month should be: freeze the formats, fix the performance overhead, run both games in CI, and have
you build one real level by hand. At the end, check the go/no-go list in §7. If it fails, move to
Godot with a clear conscience. You'll have tried the fix, and the AI tooling ideas carry over.

## 3. Measured performance (Skyforge village, 1920×1080, DPR 1, Iris Xe)

| | Thirdlight export | Same scene in bare three.js |
|---|---|---|
| WebGPU | **54–55 fps** (18 ms) | **114–152 fps** (6.5–9 ms) |
| WebGL2 | 54 fps | 137–260 fps |
| +200 animated soldiers | 38 fps | 72 fps |
| Draws / triangles | 598 / 384k | 509 / 370k |

- **It is not the GPU.** Play at half the pixels gives the same frame rate.
- **The cost per animated character is the same in both** (~0.04 ms). Crowds are affordable;
  Thirdlight's fixed base overhead is the problem.
- **Where the ~11 ms of overhead goes:**
  1. *Scene-graph bloat, ~4–5 ms.* 8,629 Object3Ds for ~800 drawn things: a wrapper Group per entity,
     1,108 LOD nodes with all their hidden levels kept attached, and statics updated every frame.
     `updateMatrixWorld` alone is 14 % of the main thread.
  2. *The worker tick runs one after the other with rendering, ~3.7 ms idle per frame*
     (`game-host/src/sim-remote.ts:143-171`). With the simulation on the main thread
     (`?threads=off`) the scene is *faster*: 62 fps.
  3. *The batcher re-groups the whole scene every frame, ~1.2 ms* (`three-adapter/src/batching.ts:322-411`).
  4. *Heavier per-draw cost, ~3 ms* (inferred, not isolated): per-object material overrides,
     16 always-present dark effect lights, 1,279 uniform buffers.
- **Your 12 fps on 2026-10-02** came mostly from three GPU defects (MSAA under the post stack, GTAO
  broken on WebGPU, an uncapped pixel ratio), fixed in the hotfix noted in plan-phase-29 (D120–D122).
  On your own laptop it is **unverified**.
- **Other timings:**
  - Editor connects in 0.9–1.2 s.
  - A command round trip is p50 47 ms / p95 61 ms.
  - Play is ready in ~1 s, then 4 s of background shader compile.
  - The export's first frame is ~1.1 s.
- **Export size:** the JavaScript is shipped **unminified with tree-shaking off**
  (`exporter/src/export-bundle.ts:24-26`): 18.6 MB raw, 5.2 MB gzip, before any content.
  It includes an unused 2D physics engine (twice) and project-model (twice).

## 5. What actually went wrong

1. **Speed over integration.** 741 commits in 18 days, overnight runs of 100+ commits, 64k lines in one
   day. The same five files were rewritten by 13–24 phases each. Nobody could keep the design in their
   head, and I can't either.
2. **Format churn with no compatibility policy.**
   - `project.json` went from v2 to v7 in 8 days; five deprecations landed in 3 days.
   - Phase 24 deleted a layer the games used: Sprout was blocked for 3 days, and ~15 % of its
     commits are engine ports.
   - Skyforge put scene work on hold for 2 days.
3. **The games are the QA department.** Of 150 defects, 25 were found by the games. Nothing in CI opens
   them.
4. **Performance was never a gate.** The GPU defects behind your 12 fps shipped because performance
   tests only ran when switched on.
5. **Built for tests, not for a person.** 18 STATUS rows still say "owner look pending". Skyforge's
   levels are built by ~15 Python scripts against the API, not in the editor. Gaps a person would hit
   in an hour (prefab links, multi-select, a fly camera) never came up.
6. **Self-imposed caps.** Script size, file count, a 64-entry trust list, 8 audio voices: each one cost
   a game days.

None of these is a three.js problem or a web problem. All of them would happen again in Godot or Unity
if the process stays the same. Engine work in Godot would just break fewer things, because there is far
less engine of our own.

## 6. Thirdlight vs. Godot, honestly, for your goals

| Your need | Thirdlight today | Godot 4 today |
|---|---|---|
| Web deploy, small and fast | Good once minified (est. ~2 MB gzip engine) | Heavier, slower start; GDScript only on web |
| Stylized 3D at 60 fps in a browser | Proven possible (bare three.js 110–150 fps); overhead fix needed | Fine (Compatibility renderer) |
| AI at the helm (edit, play, observe) | Built in; needs screenshot and typed-tool fixes | Community MCP plugins; ~2–4 weeks to match ours |
| Lego blocks + terrain + blend brushes | Blocks partial, terrain missing: phase 30 | Custom plugin on Terrain3D + GridMap/custom: weeks |
| Dialogue with voice | Works | Plugins (Dialogue Manager, Dialogic) |
| Cutscene editor | Partial | AnimationPlayer (workable) |
| Dynamic physics, prefab links, mature editor | Missing / missing / unproven | All there |
| Things breaking under you | Constantly, so far | Rarely |

The case for staying: the platform is proven fast enough, the AI loop exists, the data layer is solid,
and two games already run on it. The case for leaving: the maturity gap (physics, prefabs, terrain,
editor polish) is real and is months of work. Where Godot wins is stability, and the process change
below is meant to deliver exactly that. If it can't, Godot wins.

## 7. Recommended plan: a stabilization month, then a go/no-go

No new feature phases (28b, 29, 30 wait). In order:

1. **Freeze the formats (days).**
   - Schema v7 is the format for at least a month: no deprecations, no gameplay changes inside upgrades.
   - Upgrades get a dry-run (`tools/project.mjs check --upgrade`) and never write into game repos
     without asking.
   - Move the retry log out of game files.
2. **Games in CI (days).** On every commit, copies of Sprout and Skyforge open, Play and step for N
   seconds with no errors. Breakage is found by us, not by them.
3. **Performance overhead (~2–3 weeks).**
   - Flatten the render graph (no wrapper Groups, only the shown LOD level attached, no per-frame
     matrix updates on statics).
   - Make the batcher dirty-driven.
   - Pipeline the worker a frame ahead, or default to the main-thread sim.
   - Pool only effect lights that are actually in use.
   - Minify and tree-shake the export.
   - Target: the village at **≥ 100 fps** at 1080p on the Iris Xe, as a perf gate that is always on and
     fails the build.
4. **Fix the known defects:** visibility (E83), sun-shadow follow, script errors stopping the game
   (log and continue), slow motion past 8 catch-up steps, the substring compile check.
5. **Make the AI loop real:** screenshots as MCP images, typed tools per area, an export tool.
6. **You build one small level by hand in the editor** (an hour or two, with the things you'd normally
   do). Whatever blocks you (likely prefab links, multi-select, fly camera) is the next work.

**Go/no-go at the end of the month:**
- [ ] Village ≥ 100 fps at 1080p on the Iris Xe, and smooth on your own laptop (your eyes).
- [ ] Two weeks of engine commits with zero breakage reported by either game.
- [ ] You built a small level by hand and it felt like an editor, not a test harness.
- [ ] Export ≤ ~2.5 MB gzip of engine code.

If all four pass, plan level building (blocks + heightfield terrain + blend brushes) as the next phase,
one system at a time, each proven against a frame budget first. If they don't, move to Godot: carry the
art-factory pipeline and the AI playtest ideas across, and leave the rest.
